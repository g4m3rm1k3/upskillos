The machine reads the text one word at a time.
It counts every word, and it counts every letter.
A word that appears often tells you what the text is about;
a word that appears once tells you almost nothing.

The first programs that learned language did little more than this.
They counted which word came after which, and they used the counts
to guess the next word. The guesses were poor, but they were not random.

Later machines learned to read whole sentences at a time.
They still began where this script begins: with the text, and the counts.
